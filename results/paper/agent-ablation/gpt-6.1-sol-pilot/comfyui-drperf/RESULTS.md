The main cost variables are distinct reachable signature nodes and the input/link volume within their closures. Each requested key independently traverses its ancestry, assembles a signature for every distinct ancestor, and converts the resulting nested structure to a hashable value. Shared paths are deduplicated by order_mapping; additional links still increase input processing and nested tuple volume.

Relevant source: source/comfy_execution/caching.py:102 (batch), :112/:120 (signature assembly after ancestry), :128 (immediate signatures), :161 (ancestry visit), :55/:65 (recursive hash conversion). Observation wrappers retain the original calculations and return values. Workload behavior and session.json were unchanged.

Entry expressions are recorded in hypothesis.json. Assembly uses V=len([node_id]+ancestors), I=sum(input counts in that existing closure), and L=sum(link counts). Here I=V+L, so the independently distinguishable dimensions are V and L. Immediate signatures use local input/link counts. Ancestry uses input count and the linked-node branch H; in these particular generated graphs H also equals recursive child multiplicity. This last identity is not universal.

Source-only round 0 was submitted before measurement. Three DrPerf rounds ran six points each, all shapes at n={16,64}, then {8,24}, then {16,48}. All 18 processes passed the original workload assertion. Reports and raw measurements are saved under measurements/round-01 through round-03; analysis.py only reads saved profiles.

Distinct closure volume exactly predicts ancestry and immediate-signature call counts: 4590, 734 and 2750 across the three rounds. Recursive converter calls obey N+6*(R+E) for the generated signature structure: predictions and observations both equal 9972 in round 2 and 39156 in round 3. At equal closure size V=64, chain versus extra-edge graph hashing costs were about 5.63M versus 8.60M measured instructions, demonstrating that node count alone is insufficient.

For the whole generated batch, chains have R=N(N+1)/2 and E=N(N-1)/2; stars have R=2N-1 and E=N-1; overlapping two-parent graphs have chain R but E=(N-1)^2. These are projections of the semantic closure/input-volume interface, not workload replays used as PCVs. They explain why chain and overlapping graphs accumulate quadratic structural work while stars accumulate linear structural work, without inferring an elapsed-time law.

After adding H, ancestry has 0% reported own residual in rounds 2 and 3. Immediate signatures have 0% reported own residual in all rounds, over only three local states with correlated input/link counts. Assembly still has about 13.5% final own residual. Recursive hashing refinement uses primitive branch, sequence length, immediate container-child count and shallow child width; its residual decreases from 30.94% to 14.76%, over 101 final states. The outer hashing wrapper still has 46.55% own residual excluding its modeled child costs. No complete hash-cost interface is claimed.

Limits: annotation classification and async wrappers perturb measured instructions, especially parent regions that perform child marker-entry calculations. Rounds differ in input ranges and annotations, so residual reduction is diagnostic rather than a controlled optimization comparison. Allocation, GC, hash insertion variation and first-use effects remain unresolved. Inputs were bounded to n<=64; no extrapolated latency or coefficient law is established. Mapping objects, changing-node state, hidden IDs, non-idempotent nodes, arbitrary large input dictionaries, cycles, missing nodes and concurrent suspension were not exercised. No optimization was performed.
